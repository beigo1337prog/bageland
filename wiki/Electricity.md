Every machine has a **power node**: the socket plate in its top-left corner, the same size as an item port: **yellow** where power comes in (machines), **violet** where it goes out (generators), split yellow/violet on poles, which pass it on. A machine only runs while its node is wired, directly or through poles, to a generator that is running. Everything wired together is one **grid**; each tick the grid compares what its working machines draw with what its generators can give, and when it falls short every machine on it slows by the same share. Generators burn fuel in proportion to the load. The top bar's **Power** gauge adds up every grid: kW in use / kW available, with a bar that turns red when any grid is short.

**Steam** comes in three steps, all unlocked by **Steam Power** and none needing power:
1. **Fired Heater** (3×3, $2,500): fuel in at the back, water from the left, hot water out of the top, 10/s. Coal heats it to **300 °C** (1 coal + 20 water every 2 s); coke or petroleum coke to **400 °C** (1 lump + 40 water every 4 s). Filtered water works the same way.
2. **Boiler** (4×4, $3,500) has two loops, and both outlets must be piped somewhere:
   - **Heating loop**: hot water (any hot fluid) in at the back, 3/s. It warms the boiler towards its own temperature and leaves on the left at the boiler's temperature. With nothing hot coming in, or the heating outlet backed up, the boiler cools back to 18 °C.
   - **Steam loop**: water in from the left, 3/s, boils into **90 steam/s** at the boiler's temperature, once it is at 100 °C or more. The heating outlet sits right under the water inlet, so two pipe pieces send a boiler's heating water round to boil.
   - Filtered water in the heating loop leaves no scale: a tenth more steam (99/s).
3. **Large Steam Turbine** drinks 270 steam/s (three boilers) and needs at least half of it to turn. Two boilers give two thirds of its output; one gives nothing. One heater keeps three boilers going: about 0.45 coal/s for 1067 kW, or 0.23 coke/s for the full 1600 kW.

The spent steam leaves the turbine's front outlet as water (3 for every 90 steam, 9/s at full load), which can be piped back to the heater. With nothing on the outlet it vents; piped into something full, it backs up and stops the turbine.

**Heat**: every fluid has a temperature, set where it is made or pumped (water 15 °C, steam 300 °C, molten iron 1550 °C, molten steel 1650 °C, liquid oxygen −183 °C, liquid nitrogen −196 °C, and so on). Fluid carries its heat through pipes and tanks, mixes with what's already there, and loses a little toward the 20 °C air at each joint it crosses (a 20-piece steam line loses about 6%). A turbine's output follows its steam's heat: nothing at 100 °C or less, 1067 kW at 300 °C, and its full 1600 kW at 400 °C (never more). A boiler's steam is as hot as its heating water, so a Fired Heater burning **coke** (400 °C water) gives 400 °C steam and the full 1600 kW. A turbine takes about 6 s to spin up and runs down over 10 s when the steam stops. The inspector shows each pipe's and generator's temperature.

Every world needs power from the start. The hand crank is the bootstrap: wind it to run a water pump or a coal mine long enough to get a coal generator fed. Machines draw 5 kW (furnace) to 120 kW (electrorefinery) while working, nothing while idle. A machine's own node reaches 6 tiles and takes 1 wire, so machines can't pass power on: poles (8 wires) spread it. Generators and batteries take 3. A wire may be as long as the longer reach of its two ends.

- **Line loss**: wires lose power with length: 0.6% per tile for ordinary wire (6% over a full pole span), 0.06% per tile between two transmission towers (a wire uses the worse rate of its two ends). A machine draws its own power plus the loss along its cheapest route of wires to a generator or battery, so a machine 30 tiles of pole line away from its power needs about 18% more. The inspector shows a machine's line loss, its grid's total losses and each wire's loss.

- **Wiring**: press **C** (or **Wires** in the top bar). While wiring, the screen has an orange frame, a tag under the top bar and the Wires button lights up. Click a node to pick up a wire, then click another node to connect it. Clicking two nodes that are already wired cuts the wire. The loose wire is green when it reaches and red when it doesn't, with a dotted ring showing the reach and the hint bar giving the length or why it can't connect. Right-click or Esc lets go of the wire; again (or C) leaves wiring. With a building selected, C (or **Wire** in its inspector) starts from it.
- **Power view**: while wiring, the rest of the world dims and every power building is tinted by what it is doing: cyan generators supplying, purple generators stopped, green machines powered, orange short of power, red without power, blue idle (not drawing), pale yellow poles. Each shows its kW (`+60/150 kW` for a generator, `18/25 kW` for a machine). Every wire is drawn thicker the more it carries, with moving dots for the direction power flows and its kW written on it. A panel on the left gives total supply, demand, built capacity, the number of grids and generators, and how many machines are without power.
- **Hand crank**: hold the left mouse button (or F) on a crank and circle the mouse round it. Every half turn in one direction winds it once; going back undoes the count, and one frame counts at most a quarter turn. Since it holds only 3 s, you can keep just one crank going at a time. The handle is drawn on its own and turns: with the mouse while held, by itself while the flywheel has spin. A still crank's handle glows, and hovering a crank shows two curved arrows circling the hub.
- **Selecting** a building with a node lights up its whole grid: its wires turn bright, moving dots show power running out from the generators, other grids dim, and its reach is drawn as a ring. The inspector's **Power** box shows what it draws or makes right now, the grid's generators, machines and poles, supply against demand (now, and if everything ran at once), load, stored battery energy, and each building it is wired to with the wire's length.
- Moving a building keeps the wires that still reach; removing it drops its wires.
