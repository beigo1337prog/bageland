- **Extractors** produce their resource on any tile (Everywhere mode). In Deposits mode their speed scales with how much of the footprint sits on the deposit. Each takes the shape of the real thing: coal from a shaft mine (4×3), iron ore from an open-pit excavator (4×3), copper ore from a drill rig (3×2), sand, limestone, fluorite and borax from wide quarries (5×4, two per cycle), uranium ore from a deep mine (4×4, which needs ANFO), crude oil from an oil rig (4×3) and water from a pump (2×2).
- **Machines grow with what they do**: simple single-step machines are small (metal press and wire drawer 3×2, crusher and grinder 3×3), mid-chain plants are bigger (electrolyzer, coke oven and ore washer 4×3, smelter 4×4, caster 5×3, acid plant 5×4, oxygen converter and chemical processor 5×5) and the heavy, many-input plants are huge (copper converter 6×5, rolling mill 7×3, electrorefinery 7×5, advanced assembler 7×6, flash smelter 8×7, blast furnace 8×8). Their ports are spaced out so belts and pipes can run in side by side. Machines in saves from before the resize that no longer fit are returned to your inventory.
- **Ports**: green markers are inputs, orange markers are outputs, and a blue rim marks a fluid port. A machine has one input port per ingredient its recipes need and one output port per product, and each port carries only its own item. Inputs share one side and outputs leave from the opposite side (solids and fluids use different sides). Hover a port to see what it takes or gives and how full it is, for example `Iron Ingot 4/10`.
- **Item pipes** are enclosed, rectangular, one-way ducts. They move 2 segments/s and hold up to 4 items per segment. There are seven pieces: **Straight**, **Left Turn**, **Right Turn**, **T-Fitting**, **4-Way Junction**, **Crossing** and **Overflow Fitting**. Big yellow arrows show the flow; a piece takes items in only where its arrows point in, and sends them out where they point out. A blocked line backs up and nothing is lost.
- **Hover** over any building to see its name, status and what it is holding.
- **T-Fitting**: openings at the back and both sides. Select it and a node appears in its middle with an arrow to or from each opening. Drag from the node toward any opening (roughly that way is enough) to send items out there: a second way in turns out on its own, otherwise that opening becomes the one way out and the other two turn in. Drag an arrow into the node to turn it in (or away from it to turn it out); at least one way stays in and one out. **Tab** / **Shift+Tab** cycle the six settings: split one way in into two ways out (items take turns, skipping a blocked way), or merge two ways in into one way out. - **Crossing**: two lines cross without mixing. Select it and a node appears with a red arrow for the back-to-front line and a blue arrow for the left-to-right line; press anywhere on it and drag toward a side to send that side's line that way (**Tab** / **Shift+Tab** cycle the four settings).
- **Fluid Dump** ($250): takes any fluid from any side and throws it away at once. Dumping **Heavy Residue** (the tarry bottoms of crude distillation) costs $4 a unit. Residue also sells for −$3 at a Tanker Bay, and clings to pipes: emptying or removing a pipe or tank holding it costs $8 a unit.
- **4-Way Junction** ($80): openings on all four sides. Select it and drag from the node in its middle to an opening to make it a way out, or drag an arrow into the node to make it a way in to choose which are ways in and which are ways out, any mix with at least one of each (14 settings). A new one splits from the back out the other three; items going out take turns, lines coming in join.
- **Overflow Fitting** ($80): in at the back; items go out the front while it takes them, and only when the front is blocked do they go out the left and right in turn.
- Worlds saved before the pipe pieces are converted on load: a pipe fed from one side becomes a turn, a merge or a branch becomes a T-Fitting.
- **Machines** start in **Auto**: arriving ingredients narrow the choice to the first matching recipe, and the machine frees itself once empty. Pin a recipe in the inspector to only accept that recipe's inputs. Machines stall rather than overflow when their output can't leave.
- **Truck Depot** (selling depot) buys any item delivered to any side at that item's per-unit value. The top bar shows sales income a second, averaged over the last minute, and **Working N / M**: machines working out of all machines (the number turns amber when more than a tenth are short of input, blocked or without power). A machine's panel shows what it makes and uses a minute at full speed, what it made over the last minute, and its uptime over the last two minutes (working, short of input, blocked, no power).
- **Tanker Bay** (Selling tab, 4×3) sells any fluid piped in at the back, by the truckload: a tanker holds 600 of one fluid and drives off when full, or after 20 s with nothing coming in, and is paid for as it leaves. The next truck parks 8 s later.
- **Research Station** studies any item delivered to its sides and pays research points instead of money: 1 RP per $10 of the item's value. The first 50 of each item type a world studies pay 5× (the station lists each item's bonus units left). Points and finished topics are saved. The research tree is authored: every building past tier 1 is unlocked by exactly one topic, and a topic's prerequisites unlock the machines that make what its own buildings need (the self-test checks this). Locked buildings can't be bought, but owned spares can still be placed. Deposits only show on the map once a building that mines them is unlocked (limestone with Quarrying, fluorite with Fluorite Mining, uranium and borax with Deep Mining, crude oil with Oil Drilling); groundwater shows while a pump is in hand or selected. Holding an extractor marks the cells it can mine. The **Induction Furnace** (research Induction Melting) does the Furnace's melts on 60 kW and no coal, a fifth quicker. Tier 1 needs no research: the mines and pump, furnace, ingot molder, metal press, wire drawer, pipes, storage, the hand crank and the coal generator. Old saves are granted the research behind what they own, and get back the points of retired topics; sandbox worlds get everything, plus 999 of every building in the inventory. The window is `ResearchView` (U): 55 topics in one band per tier, each costing more than every topic before it. Researched topics are green with a check, ones you can research now have a gold border (pulsing when affordable), locked ones are dim. Each card shows what it unlocks, its cost and the time to afford it at the current rate. Picking a locked topic numbers every missing step on the way to it, with the total cost and time; **Enter** sets it as the **target**, and points then buy each step as soon as they cover it (the target's name replaces RESEARCH on the top bar). **Home** jumps to the target. The panel below lists the recipes the topic's machines run (a green dot marks a product nothing else uses), what it needs and what needs it; **1**-**9** jump to those. When the bank is nearly full (points coming in at under 5% of their value), Research Stations stop taking items and show **Bank full**, so the line backs up instead of losing them; the station's panel shows the bank and that rate. Once all 55 topics are done, the **Endless** band at the end has four topics that can be researched again and again, up to level 10, each level costing 1.6× the last: **Process Optimisation** (machines +3% speed a level, from 200,000 RP), **Logistics** (item pipes +0.25 segments/s a level, from 150,000 RP), **Grid Efficiency** (line loss -10% a level, from 100,000 RP) and **Market Contacts** (sale prices +2% a level, from 300,000 RP). They can be the target too.
- **Working animations**: a building plays a short loop of its moving parts (drill bits, fans, rams, flames, smoke) only while it is working, at game speed; anything idle, blocked or unpowered stands still. The frames are painted on demand the first time a type is seen working, cropped to the moving parts.
- **Status**: click a building (or hover it) to see its status with a coloured lamp: green working, yellow waiting for input or a recipe, red output blocked, purple no power.
- **Fluid pipes** are round, grey tubes, laid like item pipes: **Fluid Pipe** (straight), **Fluid Left Turn**, **Fluid Right Turn**, **Fluid T-Fitting**, **Fluid Joint** (four ways) and **Fluid Crossing** (two lines cross without mixing; a machine, pump or tank on one side of it connects straight through to the other). Drag a straight pipe to lay a line; corners get turn pieces, and a line started at a pump leaves it straight. R rotates a piece. Pieces join only where their openings face each other, so lines side by side stay apart. Fluid goes either way through a piece; joined pieces form one network and fluid evens out along it like pressure, and a **Booster Pump** pushes it hard along long lines. A dot of the fluid's colour shows in each filled pipe. **Tanks** (2000 units) join neighbouring tanks into one network holding one fluid. Round pipes from saves before v0.16 load as the piece with the same openings (v0.12 and v0.13 one-way pieces too).
- **Overflow Valve** (Fluid Pipes tab, $300, Fluid Handling research): fluid in at the back (the shaded end with the small chevron) goes out the front (the arrow) while the line ahead takes it; only what the front can't take spills out the left and right, in turn. Like a booster pump it splits the line, so the front and each side are separate lines, and it passes up to 10 units a tick (200/s). Use it to keep a machine's supply topped up and send the surplus to a tank or a tanker bay.
- **Split Valve** (Fluid Pipes tab, $600, tier 3, Flow Control research): fluid in at the back goes out the front and the right side in a share you set, from 0% to 100% out the side: select it, press Tab (or click the box), type the percent and press Enter (a new valve is at 50%). If one outlet is full the other takes the fluid, unless it is set to 0%. Like the overflow valve, the front and the side are separate lines, and it passes up to 10 units a tick.
- **Mixed Crossing** (Fluid Pipes tab, $40): a fluid pipe running left to right over an item duct running back to front. Fluid joins the pipes on both sides like any pipe; items go straight through from the back to whatever is in front. R rotates it.
